## What do they say, and what could I find?

<table class="claims">
<thead><tr><th>The company says</th><th>What the public record shows</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Peer-reviewed findings show electrical stimulation lowers circulating glucose, improves menstrual regularity and increases ovarian blood flow.{{ref:nr-site}}</td>
<td>Peer-reviewed trials of needle <em>electro-acupuncture</em> in PCOS exist. In a 2011 randomized trial of 84 women, menstrual frequency rose more with electro-acupuncture than with exercise.{{ref:ea-rct}} A later analysis of the same trial found no differences between groups in metabolic measures after 16 weeks.{{ref:ea-secondary}} Neither tested the LoOoP device. Ovarian blood flow: not established in what I read.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>The core technology is patented.{{ref:nr-site}}</td>
<td>The patent was granted in June 2023. Its claims describe a method of <em>making</em> tiny electrode arrays. They do not describe treating PCOS.{{ref:nr-patent}}</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>A small pilot is under way, and the company expects to file for FDA clearance in 2026.{{ref:ffh}}</td>
<td>I found no registered trial and no FDA record for the company or device.{{ref:ct-search}}{{ref:fda-search}} A pilot can exist without a public record, so this shows what I could see, not that nothing exists.</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
